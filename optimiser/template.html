Where should couriers wait?

Pick an hour: the map shows which zones are short of couriers and which have spare ones, and the optimiser moves the same couriers to cut late deliveries. By Jessica Ayivi. Evidence behind it: the Dinner Rush analysis on 538,689 real orders.

Real skeleton, simulated orders. Geography and residents (Statbel), restaurants (OpenStreetMap), rain hours (Open-Meteo, Sep–Oct 2026) and how lateness responds to courier load (Meituan's real delivery data) are real. Only the stream of orders is simulated from them, and the weekly volume is an assumption. Uber's data isn't public; nothing here is Uber data.

    Method. Load in a zone = orders × minutes with a courier ÷ 60 ÷ couriers there. Late rate = , fitted on Meituan's real delivery data: each doubling of load adds points. "Today" assumes couriers drift to where their last drop-off ended (in proportion to residents); pickups follow food places (70%) and residents (30%). The optimiser gives each next courier to the zone where it removes the most late orders, which is optimal for this curve (checked against an integer program). A simple rule, couriers in proportion to restaurants, gets within of the optimum: the gain comes from correcting drift every hour.

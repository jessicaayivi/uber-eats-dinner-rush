Food-delivery marketplace case · real data

Dinner Rush Simulator

Move the levers and see what happens to late deliveries and the weekly P&L in a real food-delivery network. An independent case by Jessica Ayivi.

Runs on real Meituan food-delivery orders: 538,689 orders in 23 business districts of one Chinese city, 17–24 October 2022, each with the delivery time promised to the customer. It is not Uber data and not Belgian data. The data has no prices or pay, so every euro figure is an assumption you can change. This research was supported by data provided by Meituan (CC BY-NC 4.0).

Levers

1Merchant fixes

Account management with the slowest 10% of restaurants: share of their pickup overrun removed.

2Fewer refusals

Better matching and acceptance nudges: share of the delay caused by couriers refusing an order removed.

3Stacking cap

An at-risk order that would be the 3rd+ in a run goes to another courier instead. Saves the measured extra last-mile time, but pays for a separate trip.

4Targeted quests

Paid extra courier-hours only where load (orders carried per busy courier) is above the trigger. 30% of spend goes to couriers who would have worked anyway.

Blanket peak bonus

Model. Each late order is late by a known number of minutes. It becomes on time if the levers save at least that many minutes. Merchant and refusal savings are each order’s measured overrun versus on-time orders in the same district and hour; the stacking saving is the measured extra last-mile time of a 3+ order run versus a 2-order run, like for like; extra couriers cut the dispatch wait and last mile along the curves measured against load (orders carried per busy courier). Lateness stops rising past a load of about : beyond it, the platform stretches promises instead. The blanket comparison pays an hour to every busy courier at 11:00–13:00 and 17:00–20:00, and 60% of the gap shows up.

How I would use this at Uber Eats Belgium. Rerun the same queries on Belgian city-hours, rank merchants by pickup overrun for account managers, and test the stacking cap and quests with a switchback experiment (city × 2-hour blocks) before paying for either.

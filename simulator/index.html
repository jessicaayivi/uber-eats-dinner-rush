Uber Eats Belgium · marketplace case

Dinner Rush Simulator

Move the levers and see what happens to lost orders, delivery reliability and the weekly P&L across Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent and Liège. By Jessica Ayivi.

Runs on a simulated marketplace (4 cities, 4 weeks, 1,568 city-hours, ~223k orders), not Uber data. The economics are anchored on Uber’s Q3 2026 Delivery figures. The method is meant to be rerun on real data.

Levers

1Rebalance shifts

Share of each stressed hour’s courier gap filled by hours moved from idle off-peak slots, at no cost.

2Batching and tighter radius

Deliveries per courier-hour when utilisation passes 85%. Today: 2.1.

3Targeted quests

Paid only on extra hours, only where utilisation after steps 1–2 stays above the trigger. 30% of spend goes to couriers who would have come anyway.

Model. Utilisation = orders ÷ (couriers online × deliveries per hour). Above 85%, unfulfilled and late rates rise along the response curve estimated from the four weeks of data. Levers act only on stressed hours (where couriers fall short of 85% utilisation). 60% of a targeted gap shows up in the blanket comparison, which pays €4 an hour to every courier on Fri–Sun dinners (17:00–22:00).
